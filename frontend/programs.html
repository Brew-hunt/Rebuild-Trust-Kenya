Programs

Four activities. One integrated model.

Each activity area feeds the others — dialogue surfaces what needs accountability, accountability needs civic education to sustain it, and all three depend on trained youth leadership to carry them county by county.

A

Citizen–Leader Dialogue

Regular, structured spaces where citizens and the people who represent them talk directly — across generations and across the usual divides.

  • Monthly citizen–leader dialogue forums
  • Intergenerational dialogue
  • Community conversations involving youth, leaders, and elders
B

Youth-Led Accountability

Young people monitoring commitments and holding structured conversations with the decision-makers responsible for them.

  • Youth-led monitoring
  • Youth accountability forums
  • Engagement between young people and decision-makers
C

Civic Education & Peacebuilding

Building the civic knowledge and preventive skills communities need before tension rises, not after.

  • Civic education sessions
  • Peacebuilding workshops
  • Preventive peace sessions
  • Awareness on peaceful civic participation
D

Youth Leadership

The backbone of the model — a trained, coordinated corps of young leaders carrying the work into their own communities.

  • Training 200 youth leaders during the pilot phase
  • County-based youth coordination
  • Youth-led community initiatives

Why This Order

Trust, Inclusion, and Safety — the three areas every activity serves.

Trust

Dialogue that holds

Citizen–leader dialogue and youth-led accountability rebuild confidence that institutions listen and respond.

Inclusion

Every generation, one table

Elders, youth, county government, and civil society are engaged as partners, not audiences.

Safety

Prevention over response

Civic education and peacebuilding workshops address tension while it is still small enough to resolve peacefully.
